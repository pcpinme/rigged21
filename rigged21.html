How This Works

This isn't a card-counting trick or a rigged random number generator — nothing is ever swapped, reordered, or adjusted mid-hand. The whole scam happens before a single card is dealt, by testing whole shuffles and only using the ones that already work out.

1. The shoe

Six standard 52-card decks (312 cards total) are combined and shuffled together, exactly like a real casino shoe.

2. The prediction

Before dealing, the game runs a full simulation of the hand off that shuffle: one card round-robin to each player then the dealer, twice — then plays out every player's turn using the exact same basic-strategy hint shown on the real table (hit, stand, or double), and finally plays the dealer's turn using the same house rule the real dealer uses (hit below 17, hit on hard 17, stand on soft 17 and above).

3. The test

The simulation checks one thing: does the dealer's resulting total beat the best surviving predicted hand at the table? (Anyone predicted to bust is already a lost cause, regardless of the dealer's total.) If yes, that exact shuffle — untouched — becomes the shoe for the hand. If no, the entire shuffle is thrown out and a fresh one is shuffled and tested again, up to 500 times, until one naturally works.

4. The deal

Whichever shuffle passes the test is dealt from the top, card by card, exactly as it came out of the shuffle. Nothing about it is touched again once play starts.

Why it actually works

The test only checks what happens if everyone plays their hint exactly. If every player does that in the real hand, they draw the identical cards the simulation already tested — so the outcome it predicted is the outcome they get, every time. At 100% Pit Boss Aggression, that means a fully compliant table loses every single hand, deterministically.

But the moment someone deviates from the hint — hits when it says stand, stands early, anything off-script — they (and everyone drawing after them from that shared shoe) start pulling cards from a part of the shoe the test never checked. The guarantee doesn't adapt to that; it just stops applying. That's why breaking basic strategy can accidentally win a hand here, even though it's normally the worse play.

The slider and the reveal

Pit Boss Aggression is simply the odds that the game even bothers running this whole search for a given round. At 0%, the first honest shuffle is always dealt, no matter what it does to anyone. At 100%, it always searches until it finds a shuffle that sweeps the table. Reveal Mode shows the exact fixed order of the current shoe and marks a rigged round's dealer cards, so you can prove all of this happened before a card was even dealt.
